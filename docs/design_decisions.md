| 13.11.2026 | Gold standard kept as planned after reconsideration: Trubar's 31 letters of the second exile (Vrečko 2015, Nos. 52–83 without the instruction No. 75); 10 for validation (one round of prompt revision allowed), 21 as test (used only once) | Extending the gold standard to the first exile (24 more letters, 55 in total, all annotated by hand) to have more data and to compare the two exiles | (1) The main question is the comparison of the two workflows. It is measured on every mentioned person and function, not on whole letters, so 31 letters already give several hundred cases. (2) 24 more letters by hand would add about three quarters to the manual work and would not fit the plan (all letters annotated by mid-December, prototype on 18.12.). (3) The first exile has different addressees (Bullinger, King Maximilian, Ungnad) and would widen the research question beyond the second exile. (4) The first exile is not lost: some of its letters serve for prompt development, its 7 letters to the estates are kept as reserve test, and a comparison of both exiles can follow later as an outlook. |

| 13.11.2026 | Elze 1897 (second exile): of the about 55 documents there that were not written by Trubar, only the 19 letters addressed to Trubar are used — their headers (sender, date, place) as incoming ties in the network, their content as background; the full-text ones are kept as reserve test | Annotating all about 55 documents not written by Trubar (letters to Trubar, letters between third parties such as the Carniolan estates, Jakob Andreae, Jurij Dalmatin or Nikodemus Frischlin, minutes of the estates) like the gold standard | Many of these documents are not letters in the full sense: about half of the letters to Trubar and many of the other pieces are Elze's own summaries in indirect speech ("he had received …"), excerpts from minutes or editorial notes, and Elze modernised the language of the rest. They are a different text type from Trubar's letters and would not be comparable in the evaluation. Annotating them would roughly double the manual work, and the research question concerns Trubar's network as seen from his own letters. The headers alone add what matters most: who wrote to Trubar. |
| 13.11.2026 | Persons count as network nodes if named or clearly referred to in the letter text; persons identified only through an editorial footnote are marked; persons who appear only in footnotes are excluded | Taking all persons from the editions' index | Footnotes contain modern scholars and context figures who are not part of Trubar's network. |
| 13.11.2026 | Letters separated by a rule-based Python script, not by an LLM rewriting the text; OCR errors in Elze 1897 left uncorrected | Letting an LLM output each letter as text and correct the OCR at the same time | An LLM that outputs the letters has to rewrite the whole text. It may modernise Early New High German spellings, "correct" forms that are right, drop or merge passages without notice, and give different results in each run. If it also corrects the OCR, I can no longer tell what is in the print and what the model changed. The script only copies characters unchanged and cuts at fixed, documented points (new page with a letter number, the word "Prevod" before the translation, the smaller font of the footnotes). It gives the same result every time and was checked against an independent text extraction. The 2015 edition needs no OCR at all (born-digital PDF); doubtful readings in Elze are checked against the scan when needed. |
